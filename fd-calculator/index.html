How FD interest is computed

Most Indian banks compound FD interest quarterly using cumulative compounding:

Maturity = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)
where P is the deposit, r the annual rate, n the compounding frequency per year, and t the tenure in years.

Frequency matters more than people assume. ₹2,00,000 at 7.1% for 5 years yields about ₹81,824 in interest with annual compounding, about ₹84,349 quarterly, and about ₹84,938 monthly. Senior-citizen rates (usually 0.50% higher) and bank-specific tenure bumps shift the result further.

Frequently asked questions

Is FD interest taxable?

Yes, fully, at your slab rate. It is taxed on accrual each year even if the FD pays out at maturity. There is no 80C benefit for interest; only 5-year tax-saver FD principal qualifies for 80C.

What happens if I break an FD early?

Banks pay interest at the rate applicable for the period actually held, minus a penalty (typically 0.5-1%). Some banks also claw back the extra interest already paid.

Are FDs insured?

Deposits up to ₹5 lakh per bank (principal + interest) are covered by DICGC insurance. Splitting large sums across banks keeps every rupee covered.

Cumulative vs non-cumulative FD?

Non-cumulative FDs pay interest monthly, quarterly, or annually instead of adding it to the principal. Maturity stays near the principal; this tool models cumulative FDs.
