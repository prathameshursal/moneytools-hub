How GST math works

Adding GST: multiply the base amount by the rate. A service billed at ₹10,000 with 18% GST carries ₹1,800 GST, so the invoice total is ₹11,800.

Removing GST: when a price already includes GST, divide by (1 + rate). The GST inside an ₹11,800 bill at 18% is 11,800 × 18/118 = ₹1,800, and the base is ₹10,000. Dividing the tax-inclusive price by 1.18 directly is the common mistake: it understates the base.

CGST and SGST: for sales within a state, GST splits equally between the Centre and the state. So 18% becomes 9% CGST plus 9% SGST. For interstate sales the same 18% is charged as one IGST line instead. The totals are identical, only the split differs.

Frequently asked questions

Is GST calculated on the discounted price?

Yes. GST applies to the net price after discounts, not the list price.

What is the difference between CGST, SGST, and IGST?

CGST goes to the central government, SGST to the state where the sale happens, and IGST (equal to CGST + SGST combined) applies on interstate sales and imports.

Do I need a GST number to charge GST?

Businesses above the turnover threshold (₹40 lakh for goods, ₹20 lakh for services, higher in special states) must register. Below the threshold, registration is optional.
