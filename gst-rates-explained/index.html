GST rates explained simply

Updated 17 October 2026 · MoneyTools Hub Team · Information only, not financial advice

GST touches every invoice in India, yet most explanations are either dense legal text or Instagram summaries. Here is the middle path.

What GST is

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a single indirect tax that replaced a stack of central excise, service tax, VAT, and entry taxes from July 2017. The core idea is "one nation, one tax" with credit flowing through the chain: a manufacturer pays GST on inputs, collects GST on output, and pays only the difference, so tax ultimately falls on the final consumer, not on business intermediaries.

The slabs, in one table

RateWhat typically sits here
0%Fresh fruit and vegetables, milk, bread, unbranded cereals, education, most health services
0.25% / 3%Rough diamonds; gold, silver, platinum and jewellery
5%Packaged foods, footwear (budget ranges), economy air travel, most transport services
12% / 18%Processed foods and phones at 12%; the huge standard band of goods and services at 18%
28% + cessCars, tobacco, aerated drinks, luxury goods; sin/luxury cess on top

The GST Council (union finance minister plus state ministers) revises assignments periodically, and recent years have moved many items downward. Treat any specific rate you read, including ours, as needing verification on the CBIC portal for invoicing.

CGST, SGST, IGST: who gets the money

For a sale inside one state, the rate splits equally: 18% becomes 9% CGST (Centre) + 9% SGST (State). For sales between states, or imports, the full 18% goes to the Centre as IGST, and the consuming state later gets its share through settlement. Union territories use UTGST in place of SGST. The customer pays the same total either way; only the ledger split changes. The GST calculator shows the split for any amount.

Two calculations everyone gets wrong

  • Removing GST from a tax-included price. GST inside ₹1,180 at 18% is ₹180, because 1,180 × 18/118 = 180. Subtracting 18% of 1,180 (₹212.40) is wrong; that overstates the tax.
  • Stacking discounts and GST. GST applies after discounts. A ₹1,000 item at 20% off is billed at ₹800 plus GST, not ₹1,000 minus tax.

Do you need to register?

Businesses supplying goods must register above ₹40 lakh aggregate turnover (₹20 lakh for services; both higher in special-category states). Some supplies require registration regardless: interstate goods supply, e-commerce selling, and casual taxable persons. Below the thresholds, voluntary registration lets you claim input credits and serve GST-registered buyers, at the cost of compliance.

Verify before you invoice: the official tariff search lives at cbic-gst.gov.in. Rates in this guide are a map, not the territory.
