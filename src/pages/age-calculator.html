How age is counted

Calendar age is trickier than subtracting years. The convention is to count full years first, then remaining full months, then leftover days, borrowing the length of the previous month when the day-of-month falls short. That is exactly what this tool does.

Watch for two quirks. People born on 29 February legally celebrate birthdays on 28 February (or 1 March) in non-leap years depending on jurisdiction. And "age at a future date" is useful beyond birthdays: visa and job applications often ask for age as of a specific cutoff date, which the as-of field handles.

Common uses

  • Checking eligibility for exams, government schemes, and senior-citizen benefits with cutoff dates.
  • Filling forms that ask age in years, months, and days separately.
  • Computing exact age at a historical or future date for legal or genealogy work.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator handle leap years?

Yes. Day counts use real calendar dates, so 29 February birthdays and leap days are counted correctly.

Why do my weeks not divide evenly into days?

Age in weeks is rounded down to whole weeks; the remainder shows up in the days figure.

Can I calculate age at a past date?

Yes. Set both fields: date of birth and the target date. The tool requires the target to be after the birth date.

Are the hour counts exact?

They are approximate because they assume whole days of 24 hours and ignore the time of day you were born.
