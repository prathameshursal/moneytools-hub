Understanding your credit score

Updated 17 October 2026 · MoneyTools Hub Team · Information only, not financial advice

Your credit score is a three-digit summary of how reliably you repay borrowed money. Lenders check it before nearly every loan and credit card decision.

What the number means

Score rangeWhat lenders typically think
750-900Excellent. Best rates, fastest approvals, negotiating power
700-749Good. Approved, occasionally at slightly higher rates
650-699Fair. Approval possible with conditions or higher pricing
Below 650Weak. Frequent rejections or expensive credit

CIBIL is the best-known bureau, but Experian, CRIF High Mark, and Equifax also operate in India, and each lender may pull a different one. Scores differ slightly across bureaus; the direction is usually consistent.

What actually moves the score

  • Payment history (biggest weight). Every on-time EMI and card bill helps; every 30+ day delay hurts, sometimes for years. One missed card payment is the most expensive small mistake in personal finance.
  • Credit utilisation. Using under ~30% of your total card limit signals control. A ₹50,000 balance on a ₹60,000 limit reads as distress even if you pay in full.
  • Credit age. Older accounts help. Closing your first (oldest) card can shorten your history and ding the score for no good reason.
  • Hard enquiries. Every loan or card application triggers a check. A cluster of applications in weeks reads as desperation; space them out.
  • Credit mix. A blend of secured (home, car) and unsecured (card) credit, handled well, scores better than only revolving cards.

Fixing a weak score, honestly

  1. Pull your report free from each bureau (one free report per year per bureau by regulation). Errors are common: loans you never took, closed accounts still open, wrong overdue flags. Dispute them on the bureau's site; fixes take 30-45 days.
  2. Clear overdues first, smallest to largest, and get "closed" status confirmed in writing where settlements were involved. A "settled" mark is better than default but worse than "closed in full".
  3. Pay card bills before the statement date, not just the due date, to keep reported utilisation low.
  4. Then wait. Scores rebuild on months of boring consistency. Anyone promising a fast score fix is selling something.
Checking your own score never hurts it. Self-checks are "soft" enquiries. Only applications trigger the "hard" kind.

Where this connects

A 780+ score is worth real money: on a ₹40 lakh home loan it can shave 0.25-0.5% off the rate, which the EMI calculator shows as tens of thousands of rupees saved over the tenure. Guard the score, and it guards your wallet.
