How the EMI formula works

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment: the fixed payment that fully repays a loan over its tenure. Lenders compute it with the standard reducing-balance formula:

EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ / ((1+r)ⁿ − 1)
where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the number of monthly instalments.

Two things matter more than people expect. First, a longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises total interest sharply: a ₹25 lakh loan at 8.5% costs about ₹17 lakh in interest over 20 years but over ₹26 lakh over 30 years, even though the monthly payment falls by only around ₹4,700. Second, because EMIs are front-loaded with interest, prepaying early saves far more than prepaying late.

Tips before you take a loan

  • Keep total EMIs under about 40% of your take-home income so surprises do not break the budget.
  • Ask for the annual percentage rate including processing fees, not just the headline interest rate.
  • Check whether prepayment carries a penalty. Floating-rate home loans in India generally do not.
  • A shorter tenure with a comfortable EMI almost always beats a longer tenure invested casually.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good EMI-to-income ratio?

Most lenders prefer total monthly EMIs below 40-50% of net monthly income. Below 35% leaves more room for emergencies and savings.

Does the EMI change during the loan?

For floating-rate loans the EMI is recalculated whenever the benchmark rate changes, unless you choose to extend the tenure instead. Fixed-rate loans keep the same EMI.

Is this calculator accurate for prepayments?

It shows the standard schedule without prepayments. Any prepayment reduces either the tenure or the EMI, and the lender recomputes the schedule at that point.

Does my data leave my browser?

No. The calculation runs locally in JavaScript. Nothing is sent to any server.
