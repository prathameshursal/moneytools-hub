What to do with your first salary

Updated 17 October 2026 · MoneyTools Hub Team · Information only, not financial advice

The first salary message hits the phone and everyone has advice: buy stocks, buy gold, buy everyone dinner. Here is a calmer sequence.

Step 0: know your real take-home

Your offer letter's CTC is a marketing number. What lands in the bank is after income tax, PF, and professional tax. Check your payslip, not the offer. If your employer contributes to EPF, note that part of your "savings" is already happening automatically. Estimate the tax slice with the income tax calculator.

The order that works

  1. Month one: park one month of expenses. Move an amount equal to one month of rent, food, and transport into a separate savings account. This is the seed of your emergency fund; it stops small shocks from becoming credit card debt at 36%.
  2. Build the emergency fund to 3-6 months. Automate a transfer on salary day so it happens before spending. Where to keep it and why it comes first: emergency fund guide.
  3. Get health insurance sorted. If your employer covers you, great, but employer cover ends with the job. In your late 20s an individual/family floater policy is cheap; buying early also means fewer exclusions later.
  4. Start one investment SIP, even ₹2,000. The point of the first SIP is not the corpus, it is installing the habit and learning how markets feel with stakes small enough to be educational. See how SIP returns work.
  5. Only then, lifestyle. Whatever is left, spend joyfully and without guilt. A sustainable plan includes fun; a punitive one collapses in month three.

Budgeting without spreadsheets

If 50/30/20 (needs/wants/savings) feels abstract, use the simpler salary-day waterfall: on salary day, money moves in this order, automatically, before you can spend it.

1. Rent and bills account → 2. Emergency fund → 3. Investment SIP → 4. Daily-spend account. What reaches the daily-spend account is genuinely free to spend.

Money moves to avoid in year one

  • Buying a car on EMI to reward yourself. A depreciating asset at 9% interest is an expensive trophy; the EMI calculator makes the total cost painfully clear.
  • Trading F&O or crypto with first-salary money. The majority of retail F&O traders lose money per SEBI's own studies. Learn with books and small index funds, not leverage.
  • Lending to every restart request. Decide a yearly gift/lend budget you can afford to lose, and stay inside it without resentment.
The one-year target: 3+ months of expenses saved, one SIP running, health insurance in place, and zero revolving credit card debt. That foundation beats any stock tip you will ever receive.
