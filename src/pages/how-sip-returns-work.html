How SIP returns actually work

Updated 17 October 2026 · MoneyTools Hub Team · Information only, not financial advice

A SIP feels like magic on investment Instagram: "₹5,000 a month becomes ₹50 lakh!" Here is what is actually happening, without the glitter.

The mechanism in one paragraph

A Systematic Investment Plan buys fund units on a fixed date every month. When markets dip, your fixed amount buys more units; when they rise, it buys fewer. This is rupee-cost averaging, and it smooths your purchase price. Separately, every unit you own compounds: fund gains are reinvested and future gains accrue on the larger base. The SIP calculator models exactly this with a fixed assumed return.

Why time does the heavy lifting

At 12% a year, a ₹5,000 monthly SIP grows roughly like this: ₹4.1 lakh in 5 years, ₹11.6 lakh in 10, ₹25.2 lakh in 15, and ₹50 lakh in 20. Notice the pattern: the last five years add more than the first fifteen combined. Compounding is back-loaded, which is precisely why starting early beats starting big, and why people who quit SIPs in year three never see the interesting part.

Three things the ads skip

  • Returns are not a straight line. The 12% is an average across violent swings. A decade-long SIP can spend a year under water. If a 30% temporary drawdown would make you panic-sell, the honest answer is to hold more debt, not to promise yourself you will be brave.
  • Inflation eats the headline. ₹50 lakh in 20 years buys what about ₹15 lakh buys today at 6% inflation. Plan in future-rupee terms for goals, then check what the corpus is worth in today's money.
  • Fees compound too. A fund charging 2% instead of 0.5% (direct plan) can shave lakhs off a 20-year corpus. Direct plans and low expense ratios matter more than most selection tips.
Realistic expectation setting: over the last two decades, diversified Indian equity has delivered roughly 12-14% annualised before fees across long windows, but individual 10-year windows have ranged from near 6% to over 18%. Treat anything above 12% as optimistic for planning.

Step-up SIPs: the quiet superpower

A flat ₹5,000 SIP ignores the raises you will get. A step-up SIP increases the amount yearly (say 10%). The same ₹5,000 start with a 10% annual step-up at 12% grows to roughly ₹79 lakh in 20 years instead of ₹50 lakh. If your income grows, a flat SIP quietly becomes a smaller share of your life.

When a SIP is the wrong tool

  • Money needed within 3 years: use an FD or debt fund; equity needs a decade to make averages meaningful.
  • Emergency savings: those belong in liquid instruments, not market-linked funds.
  • Chasing last year's best fund: recent winners mean-revert more often than not. Cost, consistency, and category fit beat star-chasing.
