The compound interest formula

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)
P = principal, r = annual rate (as a decimal), n = compounding periods per year, t = years. Compound interest earned = A − P.

Simple interest, by contrast, is P × r × t every year on the original principal only. The gap between the two widens with both rate and time: at 8% for 10 years on ₹1,00,000, simple interest totals ₹80,000 while monthly compounding produces about ₹1,21,900. The same ₹41,900 difference is why "time in the market" dominates investment advice.

Where compounding works for and against you

  • For you: FDs, RDs, PPF, EPF, debt funds, and long-horizon equity investing.
  • Against you: credit card revolving debt (24-42% annually, often compounded monthly) and unpaid loan interest. The same math that doubles your corpus can double your debt.

Frequently asked questions

What is the rule of 72?

Divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate the doubling time. At 8%, money doubles roughly every 9 years; at 12%, every 6 years.

Does more frequent compounding always mean more money?

Yes, but with diminishing returns. Moving from annual to monthly compounding at 8% adds about 0.6 percentage points of effective annual yield; monthly to daily adds almost nothing.

What is effective annual rate?

The single annual rate that produces the same result as the compounded rate. 8% compounded monthly is an EAR of about 8.30%. Compare products by EAR, not headline rate.

Does this calculator include deposits over time?

No, it models a single lump sum. For recurring monthly investing, use the SIP calculator.
