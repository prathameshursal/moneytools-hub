How BMI is calculated

BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)²
In imperial units: 703 × weight (lb) / height (in)². The formula dates from the 1830s and remains the standard population screening tool.
BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. It does not distinguish muscle from fat, so athletes often read "overweight" despite low body fat. It also shifts with age, and some health bodies suggest lower cut-offs for South Asian populations (overweight near 23, obese near 27.5) because cardiometabolic risk appears at lower BMIs. Use it as a starting point and consult a doctor for a real assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMI accurate for children?

No. Children and teens are assessed with age-and-sex percentile charts instead of the adult table above.

What BMI is healthy for Indians?

Research suggests Indian adults face metabolic risk at lower BMIs, so many clinicians target 18.5-23 rather than 18.5-25.

Can BMI be high and still be healthy?

Yes. Muscular people often have high BMI with low body fat. Waist circumference and body composition give a fuller picture.

Is my height or weight stored anywhere?

No. The calculation happens in your browser and never leaves your device.
