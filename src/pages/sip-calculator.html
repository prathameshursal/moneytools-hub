How SIP returns compound

Each monthly instalment compounds separately. The instalment made this month grows for the full period; the one made next month grows for one month less, and so on. The standard future-value formula is:

FV = P × ((1+i)ⁿ − 1) / i × (1+i)
where P is the monthly amount, i is the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the number of months.

The last multiplication by (1+i) accounts for each instalment being made at the start of the month. Because of compounding, the final years contribute most of the growth: a ₹5,000 monthly SIP at 12% for 10 years grows to about ₹11.6 lakh, but extending it to 20 years takes the same SIP past ₹49 lakh. Time in the market does more work than the amount.

Frequently asked questions

Can SIP investments lose money?

Yes. SIPs in equity funds can be below your invested amount for months or years. SIPs reduce the risk of bad timing, not the risk of the market itself.

What return should I assume?

A common conservative planning range for diversified equity funds is 10-12% annually over long periods, but actual outcomes vary widely. Debt funds are lower.

Is SIP the same as a mutual fund?

No. A SIP is a method of investing in a fund in instalments, like an EMI in reverse. You can also invest lump sums in the same fund.

Are SIP returns taxed?

Each instalment is treated as a separate purchase. Gains on equity funds held over a year are long-term capital gains taxed per current rules; check the latest rates before filing.
