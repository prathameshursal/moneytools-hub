Building an emergency fund

Updated 17 October 2026 · MoneyTools Hub Team · Information only, not financial advice

An emergency fund is boring on purpose. It is the difference between a bad month and a bad year.

How much do you actually need?

The standard advice is 3-6 months of expenses, but the right number for you depends on fragility, not on a rule of thumb. Score yourself:

  • Stable salaried job, dual income, no dependents: 3 months of expenses is defensible.
  • Single income, EMI commitments, or dependents: aim for 6 months.
  • Freelance, contract, commission income, or your industry is in hiring-freeze headlines: 6-12 months. Irregular income needs a longer runway, not optimism.

Calculate the target as monthly essential spend (rent, food, utilities, EMIs, insurance) times your chosen months. Do not include netflix-grade discretionary spending; the fund exists to cover essentials, not to fund a sabbatical in style.

Where to keep it in India

InstrumentAccess speedTrade-off
Sweep-in FD linked to savings accountInstantFD rates on idle cash with savings-account flexibility
High-yield savings account (small finance banks)InstantRates up to ~7-8%, DICGC limit of ₹5 lakh per bank applies
Liquid mutual fund1 business daySlightly better post-tax yields; small market risk
Equity, crypto, PPF lock-insDays to yearsWrong place entirely. Emergencies do not wait for market windows

A practical setup: one month of expenses in your savings account (instant), the rest split between a sweep-in FD and a liquid fund. Keep it at a different bank from your spending account so it is one impulse-purchase harder to raid.

How to build it without pain

  1. Automate on salary day. A standing instruction that moves the money before you see it. Willpower loses to automation every month.
  2. Start embarrassingly small. ₹2,000 a month is a real start. Momentum beats magnitude.
  3. Bank windfalls in halves. Bonus or gift arrives: half to the fund, half to joy. Punitive rules cause quit-outs.
  4. Define "emergency" in writing. Job loss, medical events, urgent repairs. A sale on a phone you want is not on the list, and you wrote the list for exactly this moment.

After the fund is full

Stop adding. Redirect the same monthly amount to investments (an SIP is the natural next step) or to prepaying expensive debt. Review the target once a year or whenever your expenses jump (new city, new dependent, new EMI).

The test of the fund is that it is there and untouched. If you have never dipped into it, you either have great luck or you are quietly absorbing emergencies into your credit card. Check which.
